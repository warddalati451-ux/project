2.1 — Demand

Demand = quantities consumers are willing AND able to buy at different prices. The demand curve is downward-sloping (inverse relationship — Law of Demand).

Green dashed = Increase in Demand (shift right) | Red dashed = Decrease (shift left)

Why is Demand Downward-Sloping? (3 Reasons)

1. Substitution Effect

Price ↑ → consumers buy cheaper substitutes instead.

2. Income Effect

Price ↓ → purchasing power ↑ → can buy more.

3. Diminishing Marginal Utility

Each extra unit gives less satisfaction → willing to pay less.

⚠️ KEY RULE: Changes in PRICE do NOT shift the demand curve — they cause movement ALONG the curve (change in Qd, not demand).

2.2 — Supply & Equilibrium

Supply = quantities sellers are willing AND able to sell at different prices. The supply curve is upward-sloping (direct/positive relationship — Law of Supply).

Green = Increase in Supply (right) | Red = Decrease (left)

6 Shifters of Supply P-N-T-G-E

ShifterIncreases SupplyDecreases Supply
Prices/Availability of InputsInput cost ↓ or inputs more availableInput cost ↑ or inputs scarce
Number of SellersMore firms enter marketFirms leave market
TechnologyNew tech improves efficiencyTech becomes obsolete
Govt Action (Tax/Subsidy)Subsidy → supply ↑Tax → supply ↓
Expectations of Future ProfitExpect lower future price → sell nowExpect higher future price → hold back

Equilibrium

Surplus (Qd < Qs)

Price is above equilibrium. Producers lower prices → market moves back to equilibrium.

Shortage (Qd > Qs)

Price is below equilibrium. Producers raise prices → market moves back to equilibrium.

⚠️ KEY RULE: Changes in PRICE do NOT shift the supply curve — they cause movement ALONG the curve (change in Qs, not supply).

2.3 — Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)

PED measures how sensitive quantity demanded is to a change in price. Coefficient is always negative (use absolute value).

PED = % Change in Qd ÷ % Change in Price  |  % Change = (New − Old) / Old

Inelastic Characteristics

  • Few or no substitutes
  • Necessities (gas, medicine, diapers)
  • Small portion of income
  • Required immediately
  • Coefficient < 1 (steep curve = "I")

Elastic Characteristics

  • Many substitutes available
  • Luxuries (yachts, soda, real estate)
  • Large portion of income
  • Plenty of time to decide
  • Coefficient > 1 (flat curve)

Total Revenue Test

Demand TypePrice ↑Price ↓
Inelastic (PED < 1)TR ↑TR ↓
Elastic (PED > 1)TR ↓TR ↑
Unit Elastic (PED = 1)TR unchangedTR unchanged
⚠️ Elasticity ≠ Slope. Elasticity varies along a linear demand curve — upper portion is more elastic, lower portion is more inelastic.

2.4 — Price Elasticity of Supply (PES)

PES measures how sensitive quantity supplied is to a change in price. Based primarily on time — producers need time to produce more.

PES = % Change in Qs ÷ % Change in Price  (always positive — direct relationship)

Inelastic Supply (PES < 1)

  • Hard to produce
  • High barriers to entry
  • Specialized/costly inputs
  • Hard to switch production
  • Short-run: most goods are inelastic

Elastic Supply (PES > 1)

  • Easier to produce
  • Low barriers to entry
  • Generic/low-cost inputs
  • Easy to switch production
  • Long-run: most goods become elastic
Example: Michelangelo sculptures → perfectly inelastic supply (PES = 0) — fixed quantity, can never produce more.

2.5 — Other Elasticities (XED & YED)

Cross-Price Elasticity of Demand (XED)

Measures how sensitive Qd of Good B is to a change in price of Good A.

XED = % ΔQd of Good B ÷ % ΔP of Good A
CoefficientRelationshipType
Positive (+)DirectSubstitutes
Negative (−)InverseComplements

Ex: Netflix ↑ price → Blu-ray Qd ↑ → XED = +0.25 → Substitutes
Printers ↑ price → Ink Cartridges Qd ↓ → XED = −2 → Complements

Income Elasticity of Demand (YED)

Measures how sensitive Qd is to a change in consumer income.

YED = % ΔQd ÷ % ΔIncome
CoefficientRelationshipGood Type
Positive (+)DirectNormal Good
Negative (−)InverseInferior Good

Normal: Luxury cars, seafood, jewelry (income ↑ → demand ↑)
Inferior: Top Ramen, used cars (income ↑ → demand ↓)

2.6 — Equilibrium, Consumer & Producer Surplus

Consumer Surplus (CS)

What consumers were willing to pay minus what they actually paid.

CS = Buyer's Max − Price Paid
On graph = triangle ABOVE Pe, BELOW D

Producer Surplus (PS)

Price received minus the minimum sellers were willing to accept.

PS = Price Received − Seller's Min
On graph = triangle BELOW Pe, ABOVE S

Deadweight Loss (DWL)

Lost efficiency when mutually beneficial transactions cannot take place. DWL = lost CS + lost PS. Occurs when quantity traded ≠ Qe. Total surplus is maximized at equilibrium.

2.8 — Effects of Government Intervention

Price Ceiling

Maximum legal price a seller can charge. Binding only if set BELOW equilibrium → causes a Shortage.

Goal: Make goods affordable. Result: Shortage + Black Markets. Examples: Rent control, gas price limits.

Price Floor

Minimum legal price. Binding only if set ABOVE equilibrium → causes a Surplus.

Goal: Keep prices high for producers. Result: Surplus. Examples: Minimum wage, agricultural price supports.

Excise Tax

A per-unit tax on producers. Shifts the supply curve LEFT (up) by the amount of the tax. The "tax wedge" is the vertical distance between S and Stax.

Tax Incidence — Who Pays More?

Demand vs Supply ElasticityWho Bears More of the Tax?

Perfectly inelastic supplyProducers pay ALL

⚠️ It doesn't matter whether the tax is levied on consumers or producers — the welfare effects (CS, PS, DWL, TR) are the same.

2.9 — International Trade & Public Policy

World Price (Pw)

The lower price at which countries can buy goods on the global market. If Pw < domestic Pe, it's beneficial to import.

Tariff

A tax on imports that raises the world price. Protects domestic producers but increases price for consumers.

Quota

A limit on the number of imports. Same effect as tariff (raises effective price). Purpose: protect domestic producers + prevent unemployment.

Effects on CS and PS

ScenarioConsumer SurplusProducer Surplus
No trade (domestic equilibrium)SmallerLarger

Tariff imposed (Pw + tariff)↓ Falls from free trade level↑ Rises vs free trade
